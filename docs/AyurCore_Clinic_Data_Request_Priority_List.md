# What to Ask the Clinic — In Order of Priority

A single ranked list of every ask that's come up while planning AyurCore, from "get this before you build anything" down to "don't even ask yet." The ordering reflects two things at once: how much each item unblocks, and how safe/easy it is for the clinic to say yes to — the two highest-priority items are both cheap for them to give and directly determine whether the prototype feels real or generic.

## 1. Fifteen to twenty minutes with the doctor, to record sample notes for a made-up patient

The single most useful thing you can get, and the first thing to ask for. Have the doctor talk through 4–5 typical cases exactly as they naturally would — their real mix of Malayalam and English, their real shorthand — using a placeholder name instead of a real patient. This is what tells you, before you're standing in front of them live, whether the voice and language handling actually works for this specific person. Zero privacy concerns, easy yes, directly de-risks the build.

## 2. The clinic's actual herb and formulation list

What they genuinely stock and prescribe, not the placeholder list the prototype started with. Purely operational data, no patient information at all, and it's the difference between a demo that feels tailored to them and one that feels generic. Ask for this in the same conversation as #1.

## 3. How they actually write dosage, duration, and diagnosis

Grams versus another unit, per-dose versus per-day, plain Vata/Pitta/Kapha versus combination terms or other frameworks layered on top. Small ask, same low-friction conversation as #1 and #2, and it's what makes the parsed output match their real conventions instead of forcing them to translate.

## 4. A specific doctor who'll actually run the pilot, and a window of time

Not "the clinic will try it" in the abstract — one named person, and roughly how many weeks they're willing to use it alongside their current system for real. This is the ask that turns everything above from a research exercise into an actual pilot, and it's worth getting explicit and in writing (even just an email confirming it) rather than assuming an informal nod covers it.

## 5. A sample of what a finished prescription currently looks like to them

A photo of a handwritten chit, or a printout from whatever they use now. Tells you what output format feels familiar rather than jarring when you show them the preview screen — low priority relative to the above, but easy to grab in the same visit.

## 6. Rough current stock levels for two or three of their busiest herbs

Doesn't need to be a formal audit — just enough that a "you'll run out of this in 9 days" demo moment lands as real instead of invented. Nice to have for the demo, not blocking if it's inconvenient to get.

## 7. What SaaS or system they currently use, and what actually annoys them about it

Useful competitive intelligence and a good relationship-building question, but it's fine if the answer is vague or they'd rather not get into it this early — nothing above depends on having this.

---

The rest of this list is deliberately below the line for right now — asking for any of it before the pilot has proven itself either isn't needed yet or actively works against you by raising the stakes of the conversation too early.

## 8. A written agreement covering price, support, and liability

Needed before any real patient data touches the system, not before a demo. Worth starting to draft in parallel with the pilot, but it's a "before go-live" ask, not a "before prototype" one.

## 9. Explicit sign-off that the system is cloud-hosted, not on the clinic's own machine

Same timing as #8 — this belongs in the conversation once you're talking about real patients, not the demo phase, but don't let it slide silently past that point either.

## 10. A contact for the patient consent notice

Whoever at the clinic can sign off on the plain-language notice patients see about their data being processed. Same "before real patients" gate as 8 and 9.

## 11. Anonymized historical notes, purely to study offline

Only worth asking for if #1 alone doesn't give you enough variety, and only ever framed as "let me look at patterns," never as data you're importing or storing live. Optional, and only after the pilot relationship exists.

## 12. Real historical patient records, for migration into the system

Deliberately last, and deliberately not something to ask for now at all. It's the most sensitive ask on this list, it isn't needed for the pilot to prove itself, and asking for it early signals a bigger commitment and bigger risk than the relationship has earned yet. Revisit only after the pilot has clearly worked and there's a real, written agreement in place.
